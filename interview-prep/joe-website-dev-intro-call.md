2. **What does "website" actually mean for him?** Brochure / marketing site? E-commerce? Booking / scheduling? Content + CMS? Membership / login? SaaS landing + app? "Website" covers a 10x range of effort.

7. **Who else is involved.** Partner, investor, family member, existing marketing/designer? Who decides?
8. **Maintenance expectation.** Is he comfortable updating content himself, or does he need someone long-term?

## Red flags to watch for

- **Vague budget + ambitious scope** — "I want everything Amazon has" + "I have a few hundred dollars." Gentle calibration on the call.
- **No clear customer or business model** — "I'll figure that out once the site is up." Site can't rescue a business that doesn't know who it's for.
- **Champion + committee** — decision-by-partner-by-investor-by-brother-in-law. Gets slow and scope-creepy.
- **"My cousin can do it for X"** — if he mentions a cheaper alternative, that's a signal about his real budget. Not offensive, just data.
- **Wants custom when no-code is right** — new-business founders sometimes want a custom build because it feels serious. Good consulting says no.
- **Wants a full managed service** — hosting + updates + SEO + maintenance for years. Not your shape right now.
- **Unclear what "done" means** — if he can't describe success in a sentence, the scope won't hold.
